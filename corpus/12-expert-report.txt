II. ASSIGNMENT AND SCOPE
================================================================

5. I was retained by counsel for Plaintiff Briarwood Fixtures &
Supply Co. ("Briarwood") to analyze and quantify the economic
damages allegedly sustained by Briarwood as a result of the
conduct of Defendant Cascade Industrial Components, Inc.
("Cascade") described in the operative pleadings, including
Cascade's alleged breach of the Component Supply and
Distribution Agreement dated April 2, 2022 (the "Agreement").

6. Specifically, I was asked to: (a) estimate Briarwood's lost
profits for the period from the date of alleged breach, January
14, 2024, through the reasonably foreseeable end of the
Agreement's term, December 31, 2027; (b) evaluate the
methodology and assumptions underlying Briarwood's internal
revenue projections; and (c) render an opinion as to the
present value of Briarwood's lost profits, discounted to the
valuation date of March 9, 2026.

7. My analysis and the opinions expressed herein are limited to
the economic damages issues described above. I offer no opinion
on questions of liability, causation in the legal sense, or the
proper interpretation of the Agreement, which I understand are
matters for the trier of fact and the Court.

IV. SUMMARY OF OPINIONS
================================================================

9. Based on my review of the materials described above and the
analysis set forth in this report, it is my opinion, to a
reasonable degree of economic certainty, that:

   Opinion 1: Briarwood's lost profits attributable to Cascade's
   failure to perform under the Agreement, for the period
   January 14, 2024 through December 31, 2027, total
   $6,714,300 on an undiscounted basis.

   Opinion 2: The present value of those lost profits, discounted
   to March 9, 2026 using a risk-adjusted discount rate of 9.8%,
   is $5,842,915.

   Opinion 3: Briarwood's pre-breach revenue projections were
   developed using a methodology consistent with generally
   accepted forecasting practice and are corroborated by
   Briarwood's historical growth trend and independent industry
   benchmarking data. [3]


================================================================
V. METHODOLOGY
================================================================

10. I calculated lost profits using the "before and after"
method, a generally accepted approach in forensic economics
whereby a company's actual post-breach financial performance is
compared against a "but-for" projection of what performance
would have been absent the alleged wrongful conduct.

11. To construct the but-for revenue projection, I began with
Briarwood's historical revenue under the Agreement for the
thirty-six months preceding the breach (January 2021 through
December 2023) and applied a compound annual growth rate
("CAGR") derived from that historical period. The resulting
CAGR was 7.4%, which I cross-checked against (i) Briarwood's
board-approved strategic plan, which projected 7.1% to 7.9%
annual growth, and (ii) the Fastener and Industrial Components
Manufacturers Association's published industry growth
benchmarks of 6.5% to 8.2% for the relevant product category.

12. I then subtracted actual post-breach revenue, as reflected
in Briarwood's audited financial statements and internal sales
records, from the but-for projected revenue for each year in
the damages period to arrive at lost revenue. [4]

13. To convert lost revenue to lost profit, I applied
Briarwood's historical incremental gross margin on the affected
product line, calculated as 34.2% based on a regression analysis
of Briarwood's variable costs against revenue over the 2018-2023
period (R-squared = 0.93). I deducted incremental variable costs
that Briarwood would have incurred to generate the additional
revenue, consistent with standard incremental-profit
methodology, so as to avoid overstating damages by including
costs Briarwood did not actually avoid.

14. I discounted the resulting stream of annual lost profits to
present value as of March 9, 2026 using a discount rate derived
from Briarwood's weighted average cost of capital, which I
estimated using the Capital Asset Pricing Model with a risk-free
rate based on the 10-Year Treasury Constant Maturity Rate as of
the valuation date (4.35%), an equity risk premium of 5.5%, and
an industry-derived beta of 1.15 for small-cap industrial
component distributors. [5]

reflected in the totals above.
