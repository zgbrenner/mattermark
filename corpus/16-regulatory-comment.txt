Dear Secretary of the Commission,

The Precision Equipment and Safety Alliance (PESA) hereby submits its
comments in response to the Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)
regarding Consumer Product Warnings and Labeling Requirements for Personal
Protective Equipment, published by the Consumer Product Safety Commission
(CPSC) in the Federal Register on April 10, 2026.

PESA is a national trade association representing over 340 manufacturers,
distributors, and retailers of personal protective equipment and safety
supplies. Our membership includes small businesses with annual revenues of
less than $5 million as well as major manufacturers with national
distribution networks. We have been actively engaged in product safety
compliance for more than twenty years and maintain close working
relationships with multiple federal agencies, including CPSC, OSHA, and
the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health.

PESA has reviewed the ANPRM carefully and offers the following comments
in the interest of promoting product safety while recognizing the
operational realities facing manufacturers and distributors across the
United States.


1. PROPOSED STANDARDIZED WARNING FORMATS

PESA supports the Commission's effort to create a more standardized
format for product warnings and safety notices on PPE. Consistency across
product categories will help consumers more quickly identify and
understand critical safety information. We agree that a hierarchical
labeling structure with warning severity levels (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION)
can effectively communicate risk information.

However, PESA recommends that the Commission consider the practical
constraints of PPE labeling. Many PPE items have limited label surface
area due to their small size. Requiring extensive warning text could
necessitate either significant redesign of products or reliance on
reference labels and user manuals. We propose that the final rule include
size and surface area exemptions for products with inherent spatial
constraints.

The proposed rule should specify that manufacturers may use QR codes or
augmented reality links to provide extended warning information, provided
that the primary warning statement appears in text form on the physical
product.


2. MULTILINGUAL LABELING REQUIREMENTS

The ANPRM suggests that warnings be provided in English and Spanish as a
minimum standard. PESA strongly supports this approach as it reflects the
demographics of the American workforce and will enhance safety outcomes
for a broad segment of users.

However, PESA recommends that the Commission clarify the definition of
"primary point of sale" for purposes of determining where multilingual
labels are required. For products sold through e-commerce channels,
printed catalogs, and physical retail locations, different labeling
strategies may be appropriate. We ask that the Commission provide
flexibility for manufacturers to meet multilingual requirements through a
combination of on-package labeling, point-of-sale signage, and digital
resources.


3. COMPLIANCE COSTS AND IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE

bringing products into compliance with the proposed standards. The results
vary significantly based on company size and product portfolio.

The significant range in estimated costs reflects the diversity of our
membership and the variability in product redesign requirements. PESA
respectfully urges the Commission to provide a reasonable implementation
timeline of no less than 24 months from publication of the final rule.
This period will allow manufacturers to incorporate label changes into
their routine product update and repackaging cycles, reducing unnecessary
waste and minimizing costs to consumers.

For companies with existing inventory, a phase-in period for compliance
would be appropriate. We recommend that the Commission permit the sale of
pre-compliance stock for a reasonable period following the effective date,
provided that such stock is clearly marked in accordance with transition
rules established in the final rule.


4. INTERACTION WITH EXISTING REGULATIONS

PESA is concerned that the proposed warning format standards may overlap
with existing requirements under ANSI (American National Standards
Institute) standards and OSHA regulations. We recommend that the Commission
coordinate with OSHA and relevant standards-setting bodies to ensure that
the final rule does not impose conflicting or duplicative requirements.

In particular, for products subject to ANSI Z535 standards, manufacturers
are currently required to follow specific label formats and color schemes.
If the CPSC rule prescribes different formats or colors, compliance costs
will increase substantially, and manufacturers may face legal exposure
for failure to comply with one regulatory body while attempting to comply
with another.

PESA urges the Commission to harmonize the proposed rule with existing
OSHA requirements and ANSI standards to the greatest extent possible.


5. SMALL BUSINESS CONSIDERATIONS

PESA notes that the ANPRM does not contain a detailed regulatory impact
analysis examining the effects on small businesses. In response to the
Regulatory Flexibility Act, PESA urges the Commission to perform a thorough
small business impact analysis and to consider tailored compliance
requirements for manufacturers with annual revenues below $10 million.

Small manufacturers often lack the in-house resources to redesign packaging
or modify labeling systems. They may be unable to absorb significant
compliance costs and may choose to exit certain product categories
altogether, reducing consumer choice and market competition. The Commission
should consider scaled compliance options, such as provisional compliance
pathways or technical assistance programs for small entities.


RECOMMENDATIONS

Based on the foregoing analysis, PESA submits the following recommendations
for the Commission's consideration:

1. Adopt a hierarchical warning format standard (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION)
   while permitting flexibility in label placement and size to accommodate
   product-specific constraints.

2. Specify that multilingual labeling requirements may be satisfied through
   a combination of on-package, point-of-sale, and digital resources, with
   flexibility for e-commerce distribution channels.

3. Establish a minimum 24-month implementation period from publication of
   the final rule, with a phase-in period for existing inventory.

4. Coordinate with OSHA and ANSI to ensure that the final rule harmonizes
   with existing regulatory standards and does not impose conflicting
   requirements.

5. Conduct a comprehensive regulatory impact analysis under the Regulatory
   Flexibility Act and establish scaled compliance options for small
   manufacturers.

6. Establish a technical assistance program to help small and medium-sized
   manufacturers understand and implement the new labeling requirements.

7. Permit the use of QR codes and digital labeling platforms as
   supplementary means of providing extended warning information, provided
   that primary warnings remain in text form on the physical product.

PESA appreciates the Commission's consideration of these comments and
welcomes the opportunity to discuss them further. We are committed to
working with the Commission to develop practical and effective labeling
standards that promote consumer safety while recognizing the operational
constraints facing manufacturers across the nation.

Please contact the undersigned if you have questions regarding these
comments or if you wish to schedule a meeting with PESA representatives
to discuss these issues in greater detail.
