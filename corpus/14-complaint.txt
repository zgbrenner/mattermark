              COMPLAINT AND DEMAND FOR JURY TRIAL

     Plaintiff Halverson Ridge Capital Partners, LLC ("Halverson
Ridge") sues Defendants Dunmore Lattice Technologies, Inc.
("Dunmore Lattice") and Wendell A. Prichard ("Prichard," with
Dunmore Lattice, "Defendants") and alleges as follows:

                          INTRODUCTION

     This action arises from Defendants' breach of a $4,200,000
convertible note financing and related misrepresentations that
induced Plaintiff's investment in Dunmore Lattice, a Boulder,
Colorado manufacturer of data-center cooling lattices. Defendants
misrepresented the company's revenue backlog, concealed senior
secured debt, diverted restricted proceeds, and breached
contractual and fiduciary duties, causing damages exceeding
$5,000,000.

                             PARTIES

     1.   Plaintiff Halverson Ridge Capital Partners, LLC is a
Delaware limited liability company with its principal place of
business in Denver, Colorado. Its sole member is domiciled in
New York.

     2.   Defendant Dunmore Lattice Technologies, Inc. is a
Colorado corporation with its principal place of business in
Boulder, Colorado.

     3.   Defendant Wendell A. Prichard is, upon information and
belief, a citizen of Colorado residing in Boulder County, and at
all relevant times was CEO and a director of Dunmore Lattice.

     4.   Prichard acted both individually and as an officer and
agent of Dunmore Lattice, and his acts below were within his
authority and ratified by the company.

                    JURISDICTION AND VENUE

     5.   This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. Section
1332(a)(1) because the parties are completely diverse and the
amount in controversy exceeds $75,000.

     6.   This Court additionally has jurisdiction under 28
U.S.C. Section 1331 to the extent any claim arises under federal
law.

     7.   This Court has personal jurisdiction over Defendants
because Dunmore Lattice is incorporated and headquartered in
Colorado, Prichard resides in Colorado, and the alleged conduct
occurred substantially in Colorado.

     8.   Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. Section 1391(b)(1) and
(b)(2) because Defendants reside in this District and a
substantial part of the events alleged, including execution of
the Note Purchase Agreement described below, occurred here.

                     FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

     9.   Halverson Ridge is a private fund making growth-stage
debt and equity investments in industrial technology companies.

     10.  In January 2025, Prichard approached Halverson Ridge on
behalf of Dunmore Lattice seeking growth capital.

     11.  From January to March 2025, Prichard held numerous
calls and meetings with Halverson Ridge, presenting financial
projections and backlog data.

     12.  On February 11, 2025, Prichard provided a "Confidential
Investor Summary" representing a signed customer backlog of
$18,400,000 for fiscal year 2025.

     13.  The Investor Summary also represented a trailing
twelve-month gross margin of 41% and no debt senior to the
proposed note.

     14.  In reasonable reliance on paragraphs 12 and 13,
Halverson Ridge agreed to finance Dunmore Lattice.

     15.  On March 24, 2025, the parties executed a Note Purchase
Agreement under which Halverson Ridge purchased a convertible
note in the principal amount of $4,200,000 (the "Note").

     16.  Prichard executed the Note Purchase Agreement for
Dunmore Lattice and separately executed a Founder Support and
Representations Agreement (the "Support Agreement") personally
reaffirming the Investor Summary.

     17.  Section 3.2 of the Note Purchase Agreement restricts
use of proceeds to working capital, equipment, and general
corporate purposes in the board-approved budget.

     18.  Section 4.6 warrants a signed backlog "not less than
$16,000,000" and "no Senior Indebtedness other than as disclosed
on Schedule 4.6." Schedule 4.6 disclosed none.

     19.  Section 5.1 of the Support Agreement requires Prichard
to promptly disclose any event rendering a representation
inaccurate.

     20.  Section 6.4 obligates Prichard to provide accurate
quarterly reporting to Halverson Ridge's board observer.

     21.  In April 2025, Dunmore Lattice received the $4,200,000
Note proceeds.

     22.  Beginning in May 2025, board observer Corinne Vasquez
received monthly financial packages reflecting a backlog of
$17,900,000, then $17,200,000 in June.

     23.  In July 2025, Ms. Vasquez requested supporting purchase
orders under Section 7.3; Dunmore Lattice did not produce them in
July or August 2025.

     24.  On September 9, 2025, at Prichard's direction, the
controller produced purchase orders totaling only approximately
$6,100,000, roughly $10,300,000 less than represented.

     25.  Halverson Ridge learned much of the reported backlog
consisted of non-binding letters of intent, not signed orders.

     26.  Prichard knew, or was reckless in not knowing, that the
reported backlog included non-binding letters of intent when he
made the representations above.

     27.  In September 2025, a UCC search revealed Dunmore
Lattice granted a security interest in substantially all its
assets to Meridian Commercial Lending Corp. in February 2025.

     28.  The Meridian interest was not disclosed on Schedule 4.6
or otherwise before closing, secures a $2,750,000 loan, and is
senior to the Note by its terms.

     29.  Halverson Ridge's Note is therefore structurally
subordinated to debt Defendants represented did not exist.

     30.  On October 3, 2025, Halverson Ridge demanded a written
accounting of the use of the Note proceeds.

     31.  On October 21, 2025, Dunmore Lattice's partial
accounting showed approximately $1,650,000 transferred to
Prichard Family Holdings LLC, an entity Prichard, upon
information and belief, wholly owns and controls.

     32.  The transfers were not for working capital, equipment,
or any purpose in the board-approved budget.

     33.  Upon information and belief, Prichard used part of the
transferred funds for personal debts unrelated to Dunmore
Lattice.

     34.  The accounting did not address the remaining
approximately $840,000 of Note proceeds.

     35.  On November 12, 2025, Halverson Ridge sent written
notice of default under Section 8.1, demanding cure within the
thirty-day period under Section 8.3.

     36.  Defendants did not cure the defaults within that period
or at any time thereafter.

     37.  On December 15, 2025, Halverson Ridge accelerated the
Note under Section 8.4, declaring the full $4,200,000 principal,
plus accrued interest, immediately due and payable.

     38.  Dunmore Lattice has not repaid any portion of the
accelerated Note.

     39.  As a direct and proximate result of Defendants'
conduct, Halverson Ridge has been damaged in an amount to be
proven at trial, believed to exceed $5,000,000.

     40.  Halverson Ridge performed all conditions and
obligations required of it under the Note Purchase Agreement and
the Support Agreement, except as excused by Defendants' breaches.

     41.  As an officer, director, and personal reaffirming party
under the Support Agreement, Prichard owed Halverson Ridge duties
of honesty, good faith, and fair dealing.

     42.  Prichard breached those duties by causing the
misrepresentations above, authorizing the undisclosed Meridian
interest, and directing diversion of Note proceeds to Prichard
Family Holdings LLC for his personal benefit.

     43.  Dunmore Lattice's board did not approve the transfers
to Prichard Family Holdings LLC described in paragraphs 31
through 33.

                              COUNT I
                       BREACH OF CONTRACT
                    (Against Dunmore Lattice)

     44.  Halverson Ridge realleges paragraphs 1 through 43 as if
fully set forth herein.

     45.  The Note Purchase Agreement is a valid, enforceable
contract, and Halverson Ridge performed by funding the full
$4,200,000 Note.

     46.  Dunmore Lattice breached Sections 3.2, 4.6, 7.3, and
8.1 by misrepresenting its backlog and debt position, failing to
produce documentation, misusing proceeds, and failing to cure
after notice.

     47.  As a direct and proximate result, Halverson Ridge has
suffered damages of not less than $4,200,000 in unpaid principal,
plus accrued interest and consequential damages.

     48.  Halverson Ridge is entitled to its damages, pre- and
post-judgment interest, costs, and, under Section 9.7 of the Note
Purchase Agreement, its reasonable attorneys' fees.

                             COUNT II
                                FRAUD
                     (Against All Defendants)

     49.  Halverson Ridge realleges paragraphs 1 through 48 as if
fully set forth herein.

     50.  Defendants made material misrepresentations of existing
fact, including as to Dunmore Lattice's signed backlog and the
absence of senior debt.

     51.  Those representations were false when made, and
Defendants knew they were false or made them with reckless
disregard for their truth.

     52.  Defendants intended Halverson Ridge to rely on the
representations in deciding to provide the financing, and
Halverson Ridge reasonably and justifiably did so.

     53.  As a direct and proximate result, Halverson Ridge has
suffered damages believed to exceed $5,000,000, entitling it to
compensatory damages plus punitive damages sufficient to punish
and deter Defendants' willful conduct.

                             COUNT III
                  BREACH OF FIDUCIARY DUTY
                        (Against Prichard)

     54.  Halverson Ridge realleges paragraphs 1 through 53 as if
fully set forth herein.

     55.  As alleged in paragraph 41, Prichard owed fiduciary and
related duties of honesty, loyalty, and fair dealing to Halverson
Ridge.

     56.  Prichard breached those duties by causing the
misrepresentations, authorizing the undisclosed Meridian
interest, and diverting at least $1,650,000 in restricted Note
proceeds to Prichard Family Holdings LLC for his own benefit, in
violation of Section 3.2.

     57.  Prichard's breaches were not the result of good faith
business judgment but were undertaken for his personal benefit to
the detriment of Dunmore Lattice and Halverson Ridge.

     58.  As a direct and proximate result, Halverson Ridge has
suffered damages believed to exceed $1,650,000, plus consequential
damages from the resulting impairment of its Note.

     59.  Prichard's conduct was willful, malicious, and in
conscious disregard of Halverson Ridge's rights, entitling
Halverson Ridge to punitive damages.

                       PRAYER FOR RELIEF

     WHEREFORE, Plaintiff Halverson Ridge Capital Partners, LLC
respectfully requests that the Court enter judgment against
Defendants and grant the following relief:

     a.   Compensatory damages against Dunmore Lattice on Count I
          of not less than $4,200,000, plus pre- and
          post-judgment interest;

     b.   Compensatory damages against all Defendants on Count II
          believed to exceed $5,000,000;

     c.   Punitive damages against all Defendants on Count II;

     d.   Compensatory damages against Prichard on Count III
          believed to exceed $1,650,000;

     e.   Punitive damages against Prichard on Count III;

     f.   Reasonable attorneys' fees and costs of suit to the
          extent permitted by the Note Purchase Agreement or
          applicable law;

     g.   Pre-judgment and post-judgment interest at the maximum
          rate allowed by law; and

     h.   Such other and further relief as the Court deems just
          and proper.
